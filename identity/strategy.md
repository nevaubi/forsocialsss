# strategy.md

What we are building and for whom. Tier B file: edits only during retro with diff to Slack, or by direct instruction from Firas (logged in lessons.md).

## Position

The AI architect who ships production agentic systems inside high-stakes regulated domains: law, finance, immigration, medical. Most AI content is either research commentary or indie-hacker demo reels. Firas's lane is the unglamorous middle: what it takes for agents to survive compliance, audits, scale, and real users. The lane is specific, credible, and thin on quality voices. Own it.

## Audience, in priority order

1. AI engineers and architects building agentic systems. Peers. They validate quality, comment substantively, and share.
2. Legal ops, legal tech, and innovation leaders. Buyers and employers in the flagship domain.
3. Fintech and research-tooling builders. DeltaSage adjacency.
4. Founders and hiring managers evaluating Firas through his body of work.

## Pillars (rebalanced 2026-08-02 by founder directive: more architecture, more trending AI news, more legal AI)

- P1, Legal AI and regulated-domain production systems (flagship, ~40%): litigation intelligence engineering, compliance-aware patterns (RBAC, immutable audit trails, ZDR inference, Bedrock), legal AI market moves analyzed through a builder who actually ships inside a law firm, war stories at pattern level. Never client-specific.
- P2, AI architecture and agentic engineering (~35%): system architecture takes, harness and loop engineering, context and memory design, evals and observability, orchestration, cost control. The published Loop Engineering post lives here; build on it.
- P3, Trending AI news through a builder's lens (~20%): model releases, agent platform shifts, security incidents, notable papers. The rule: react within 24-48 hours of the news or skip it, and every reaction must carry an engineering take or a production implication. Summary-only news posts are banned.
- P4, Builder's log (~5%): DeltaSage and venture lessons, selectively. Substance-first, no hustle theater.

## Cold-start reality (42 followers at setup)

The 2026 feed weights topical expertise and interest-graph relevance over raw follower count, which favors this account. Nothing distributes without initial engagement, though. The plan:

- Consistency first. Hold the one-post-per-two-days average for six weeks before judging anything.
- Depth formats. The 30-page document instinct was right: documents and dense text posts maximize dwell, which is the dominant ranking signal.
- Comment leverage. The agent surfaces 3 to 5 high-fit comment opportunities per week (tracked-creator posts where Firas has a genuinely additive point) in the weekly Slack digest. Firas comments manually, in his own words. At this account size, substantive comments on larger accounts are the primary discovery channel.
- Profile conversion. Content stays consistent with the headline so profile visits convert to follows.
- No shortcuts. No pods, no follow-churn, no bait, no borrowed reach via tag spam. Reach is earned or not taken.

## Cadence policy

Rolling 14-day target of 7 posts. Minimum 20 hours between published posts. The quality bar overrides everything; holds are communicated in Slack, not hidden.

## What winning looks like

- Days 0-30, process health: 10+ drafts delivered, at least 60% approved with light edits, zero never.md violations, calibrated predictions starting to form.
- Days 30-90, audience signal: comments from target-audience roles, profile views trending up, at least 2 inbound conversations traceable to posts.
- Days 90+, compounding: follower slope positive, saves and reposts on P1/P2 posts, retro-validated playbook entries replacing priors.

Engagement counts are calibration data, not the goal. The goal is a public body of work that reads like the engineer he is.
