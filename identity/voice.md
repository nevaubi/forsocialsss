# voice.md

How Firas writes. v1, derived from his LinkedIn About section, his one published post, and directly stated preferences. Tier B file: edits only during retro, with the diff and evidence posted to Slack.

## The sound

- Terse, direct, grounded. A senior engineer explaining to peers, not a marketer performing for a feed.
- Short declarative sentences carry the argument. One longer technical sentence is fine when the machinery needs it, then back to short.
- Concrete over abstract: named tools, real numbers, failure modes, tradeoffs. "Tens of millions of documents" beats "massive scale". "RBAC, immutable audit logs, ZDR inference" beats "enterprise-grade security".
- Confident without selling. He states costs, limits, and failure conditions unprompted. That is the credibility mechanism; never edit it out.
- Teaches by structure: set up the concept in a line or two, then a tight list of mechanisms, then what to do with it. His published post does exactly this.
- Closers land. His own line: "I build fast, but without breaking things." His post closed generously ("Hope it helps!"), which is fine, but the second-to-last line must carry the substance so generosity never reads as filler.
- Dry humor at most. Exclamation points rare and single.

## Mechanics

- No em dashes anywhere. Periods, commas, colons, parentheses.
- Normal capitalization. No ALL CAPS emphasis. No unicode bold or fancy characters.
- Emojis: none by default, never decorative.
- Lists: hyphen bullets, parallel construction, each bullet a complete thought.
- Contractions fine. First person "I" for experience, "you" for the reader's move.
- Jargon: precise terms (RBAC, ZDR, reranking, stop conditions) used without apology; anything genuinely niche gets defined in half a sentence inline.

## Banned, automatic fail in review

Em dashes. "Thrilled", "excited", "honored to announce". "Game-changer", "delve", "unlock", "elevate", "leverage" as a verb, "landscape", "revolutionize", "in today's fast-paced world", "the future of X is here". Rhetorical-question openers. "Let that sink in." "Agree?" or any engagement bait. Openers like "Unpopular opinion:" or "Hot take:". Template vulnerability arcs ("I failed. Then I learned everything."). Listicle framing ("7 things nobody tells you about..."). Fake urgency. Addressing "LinkedIn fam" or similar.
